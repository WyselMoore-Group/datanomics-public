# Datanomics

**Owner:** WyselMoore Holding Co Pty Ltd (ACN 683 739 273), trading as WyselMoore Group  
**Maintained by:** Matthew Wysel  
**Visibility:** Public

Datanomics is the economic science of how data and knowledge create,
transfer and accumulate value. It connects people, value and data. People
set the goal, trade the knowledge, and judge the value; systems —
computers, algorithms, processes — automate much of the trading. A search
for "hotels near me" is traded for a list of hotels. A heartbeat is traded
for a health check. A question to an AI is traded for an answer. Each of
those is a knowledge trade, and each has a price that nobody wrote down.

This repository is the public record of the field: its vocabulary, its
founding research, and an operational framework derived from it.

---

## Everyday datanomics

Every organisation already operates a knowledge market. Each decision,
conversation, AI interaction and data flow is a trade: something known
moves from one party to another, and both act differently afterwards.
Those trades settle in units no ledger records — time, access, obligation,
standing — so the market that settles them appears nowhere in the accounts.
It runs without prices, without governance, and without anyone responsible
for it.

What is not recorded is not managed. What is not managed is priced by
default, and the default price is the extraction price: what an asset can
be sold for, or made to save, once. Knowledge is not that kind of asset.
Its value lies in what it can be recombined with, and that value is forgone
on both sides of every trade priced at extraction. The loss never appears
as a loss. It appears as AI investment that fails to compound, as data
strategy that describes infrastructure rather than return, and as decisions
taken on instinct inside firms that already hold everything needed to take
them otherwise.

## The mechanism

There are four concepts that anchor the field.

**Data, information and knowledge are non-rival. What sharing costs is
control, not the asset.** One party's use does not consume the asset, whichever
layer it sits at. But a share cannot be recalled: once data is disclosed,
control over who holds it is shared too. Goods-dominant logic reads that
concession as depletion and prices the trade as a transfer. It is not one.
Under service-dominant logic the shared stock, and each party's return from it,
grow with supply — and knowledge, because it recombines, returns options to both
sides that neither party held before. Most organisations price all three as if
sharing were a loss.

**Value is constituted by uncertainty, not by use.** Reverse the direction
of Shannon's communication system and the coupling between uncertainty and
value is present in the data before transmission, and before anyone has
decided what the data is for. Knowledge can therefore be valued before,
and independently of, whatever is eventually done with it. Valuation by
realised use returns a figure that is correct and useless.

**Knowledge pays in parallel, and every appraisal instrument counts it
once.** One investment can be sold, used to improve other products, and
incorporated as learning — at the same time, from the same act, without
depletion. Every appraisal instrument a firm owns assumes a rival asset
returning a single stream, so applied to knowledge it understates
directionally, systematically, and most severely on the holdings that
compound. The spread between extraction value and extensibility value is
that understatement, and it has a number.

**Sharing is the rational act, and its value is conditional on how much
and how often.** Where the worth of a shared stock depends on supply level
and supply frequency rather than on any single contribution, the Pareto
frontier of a data sharing ecosystem expands with supply and oversharing
becomes the rational strategy. Where governance is absent, the same
ecosystem degrades on the pattern of a commons. Knowledge market failure
has a form, a diagnostic, and a correction.

## The background

Datanomics stands on platform economics, the economics of information,
Shannon's communication theory, club theory, service-dominant logic, and
the study of data as a production factor and an exchangeable service. In
short: datanomics treats data — and knowledge — as an asset that is
tradeable.

It adds to that literature a value creation process for data, a
firm-level production function that carries non-rivalry and parallel
payoffs, a market-level exchange model in which oversharing is rational,
and a specification of market failure in shared data resources.

The term was coined in Wysel, Baker and Billingsley (2021), published in
*Agricultural Systems*, as "agricultural datanomics". It was generalised
in Wysel (2023), *Data Sharing Ecosystems and the Creation of Value from
Data*, a thesis by publication at the University of New England. The
empirical setting throughout is a functioning data sharing ecosystem in
cattle genetics, chosen because its trades were observable and its
failures had already occurred.

## Neighbouring fields

Datanomics sits beside several fields and practices. A reader who knows
one of them should be able to see the boundary from here.

The economics of information (Arrow; Stigler; Shapiro and Varian)
established that information is costly to produce, cheap to reproduce,
and traded in markets that behave unusually. Jones and Tonetti (2020)
formalised the non-rivalry of data at the level of the economy and showed
that who holds the property right determines whether data is over- or
under-used. Datanomics takes non-rivalry as given from that work and asks
the question beneath it: what a firm, a platform and a community must do
for value to arise from a non-rival asset at all, and how that value is
priced when it is traded.

Infonomics (Laney) argues that information should be valued and accounted
for as an asset. Datanomics agrees on the destination and differs on the
mechanism. Value is not a property the asset carries onto a balance sheet;
it is a result produced by a process, and produced in parallel, which is
why single-line asset valuation understates it.

Knowledge management (Nonaka and Takeuchi; Davenport and Prusak) governs
the stock of knowledge: how it is captured, stored, retrieved and converted
between tacit and explicit form. Datanomics governs the trade: what
happens to value when knowledge moves between parties. A firm can run
excellent knowledge management over a failing knowledge market.

Knowledge systems — repositories, graphs, retrieval, and the models that
now sit over them — are the asset layer of a data sharing ecosystem.
Datanomics holds that the asset layer is necessary and not sufficient.
Value arises only where the asset, management and output layers are all
present, and a system deployed without the other two produces inventory,
not return.

The word "datanomics" has been used informally, as a company name or a
label for data-driven business, before and since 2021. Wysel, Baker and
Billingsley (2021) is where it was given a definition, a model and an
empirical test, and it is used here in that sense.
